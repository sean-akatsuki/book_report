After 10 years working directly in the tech industry, switching to the IT department of an insurance company two years ago was a massive reality check.

It taught me a valuable lesson from a completely new perspective: Most non-IT companies don't naturally "think" in IT.

To them, technology isn't a core strategy—it’s just a tool to be evaluated, purchased, and measured against traditional business goals.

Having seen both sides, here are 3 key observations:

1️⃣ Business values drive IT values

In a non-tech enterprise, the company’s core mission dictates what every department pursues. Technology exists to serve existing business logic, not to rewrite it overnight.

2️⃣ Tools over mindsets

Non-tech organizations rarely adopt "IT-native" methodologies automatically. They tend to evaluate software like office equipment: What does it cost, and what does it fix right now?

3️⃣ The role of tech partners

Helping non-IT clients succeed requires more than just selling features—it takes customer education and helping them build a tech-oriented mindset. While I believe many top IT service companies are already putting this into practice as a standard, seeing it from the client side really reinforced how crucial it is.

Looking back over my decade in tech, this shift in stance has been one of my biggest learning moments. It completely changed how I understand the bridge between technology and business.

💡 For my network: From your experience, what’s the most effective way to help traditional organizations move from "buying a tool" to "adapting a mindset"?
