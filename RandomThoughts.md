Realizing this after 2 years in an insurance company’s IT department(Japan Market):

Most non-IT companies don't actually "think" in IT.

To them, technology isn't a core strategy or a mindset—it’s just a tool to be evaluated, purchased, and measured against traditional business goals.

Here are 3 key takeaways from my experience:

1️⃣ Business Values Drive IT Values
In a non-tech enterprise, the company’s core value defines what every department pursues—including IT. Technology exists to serve existing business logic, not to rewrite it overnight.

2️⃣ Tools Over Mindsets
Non-tech organizations rarely adopt "IT-native" methodologies naturally. They evaluate software like buying office equipment: What does it cost, and what does it fix right now?

3️⃣ The Real Challenge for Tech Vendors
If you run an IT company selling to non-tech enterprises, selling features isn't enough. Your real job is customer education. You have to teach them how to think with tech, not just how to use it.
